Interleaving is worth testing — but placement and purification power must be separated.

The proposed noise-then-purify versus noise/purify/noise/purify comparison is the right baseline for the open question in the manuscript. A second, resource-matched comparison is required to tell whether any gain comes from where purification is placed, rather than simply from applying it more often.

What the manuscript says

Sections III.D and IV.B show that ideal common unitary blocks can be pushed through the SWAP tree. The manuscript then explicitly leaves genuine circuit-level schedule advantage as an open problem because preparation, memory, SWAP-test, and reset noise can make placement matter. The proposed study is therefore a useful ideal-channel baseline, not yet a full circuit-level answer.

A · Literal policies

Different power
end = P ∘ ET
interleaved = (P ∘ E)T

This answers the operational question “should I purify once or after every noise block?” It is useful, but the interleaved arm has T purification rounds while the end arm has one.

Keep as panel A

B · Matched placement

Primary result
end = PT ∘ ET
interleaved = (P ∘ E)T

This holds the number of purification layers fixed and isolates schedule placement. Report ΔF = Finterleaved − Fend; positive values are genuine interleaving gains.

Use as headline comparison

A quick sanity check changes the expected story

Using the repository’s untwirled one-qubit Z-dephasing map with |+⟩, p = 0.1, and T = 5: raw F = 0.66384; one purification at the end gives 0.79591; purifying after every step gives 0.98414. But with five purification rounds at the end, the matched-budget end schedule gives 0.9999999997 — higher than the interleaved result. This is only a smoke calculation, but it demonstrates why the matched control is decisive.

Physics and implementation guardrails

Confound

Purification power

One round after every noise step is not comparable to one round at the end.

Interpretation

Ideal map only

This tests nonlinear map placement, not noisy SWAP hardware or signed-estimator variance.

Physics

Target choice

|+⟩ isolates spectral recovery; an off-axis product state reveals the dephasing eigenvector tilt.

Schema

P_success mismatch

The two active rho² packages use different P_success semantics. Store purity explicitly instead.

Recommendation

Proceed with the study as a small standalone module. Treat the literal two-arm trajectory as an operational comparison, and make the matched-budget end-versus-interleaved result the scientific claim. Begin with untwirled dephasing; add twirling and realistic SWAP/reset/memory errors only after this baseline is stable.

Based on the 25-page manuscript, the active rho² code paths, stored CSV schemas, existing rho² figures, and a small direct-map sanity check. No simulation source files were changed.
